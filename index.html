Building blocks

nihon (Japan) + jin (person) = nihonjin, Japanese person. The same -jin works everywhere: Indo-jin, Amerika-jin.

no means of, so watashi no is literally "I of", meaning my.

Particles

The small words that mark what role each thing plays in a sentence.

ParticleJobExample
wa はMarks the topic, "as for…"Watashi wa tabemasu, I eat
ga がMarks the subject, points at itWatashi ga nihonjin desu, I'm the Japanese one
no のOf. Possession, connectionNihon no anime, Japanese anime
o をThe object, what you do it toGohan o tabemasu, I eat rice
ni にTime, destination, personGakkou ni ikimasu, I go to school
de でPlace of action, or methodIe de tabemasu, I eat at home
to とWith, andTomodachi to ikimasu, I go with a friend
kara からFromTokyo kara kaerimashita, I came back from Tokyo
wa versus ga

wa points away from the topic, towards what is said about it. ga points back at the subject, singling it out.

Watashi wa nihonjin desu: I am Japanese. An ordinary statement.
Watashi ga nihonjin desu: I am the Japanese one. Said when someone assumed it was your friend.

ni versus de

Kouen ni ikimasu
I go to the park (ni, because the park is the destination)
Kouen de asobimasu
I play in the park (de, because the playing happens there)
Shichi-ji ni okimasu
I wake up at 7:00 (ni for a point in time)
Densha de ikimasu
I go by train (de for the method)
Densha de Tokyo ni ikimasu
I go to Tokyo by train, both at once

Stacking them up

Start with a bare sentence and keep adding to it.

Watashi wa sushi o tabemasu
I eat sushi
Kyou wa shichi-ji ni sushi o tabemasu
Today I'll eat sushi at 7:00
Watashi wa tomodachi to tomodachi no ie de nihongo o benkyou shimasu
I study Japanese with a friend at my friend's house
Watashi wa juu-ji ni okaasan to kutsu to fuku o mooru de kaimasu
At 10:00 I'll buy shoes and clothes with my mum at the mall
Konshuu no doyoubi wa tomodachi to roku-ji ni haikingu ni ikimasu
This Saturday I'll go hiking with a friend at 6:00
Word order is freer than English

Once the particles are doing their job, the pieces can move around. Watashi wa shichi-ji ni sushi o tabemasu and Watashi wa sushi o shichi-ji ni tabemasu are both fine. The verb is what cannot move. It always goes at the end.

Months that break the pattern

April is shi gatsu (not yon), July is shichi gatsu (not nana), September is ku gatsu (not kyuu). Three exceptions, worth learning as a set.

And the 20th is hatsuka, not juu-ni-juu-nichi.

Verbs: the three groups

Every verb belongs to one of three groups. The group decides how it changes, so learning the group once makes every later form follow.

Group 1: ru verbs

End in ru. Drop the ru, add masu.

taberuto eattabemasu食べる → 食べます

Verbs that end in ru but are not Group 1

Some verbs end in ru and still belong to Group 2: kaeru (come back), suwaru, hashiru. These have to be learned one by one, because the spelling will not tell you.

There are also two different kaeru. 変える, to change, is Group 1 and becomes kaemasu. 帰る, to come back, is Group 2 and becomes kaerimasu.

Attach shimasu to a noun and you have a verb. Souji o shimasu, I clean. Ryouri o shimasu, I cook. Benkyou o shimasu, I study. Kaimono o shimasu, I shop.

Verb or noun?

Several words pair up. Kaimasu is the verb to buy, kaimono is the noun shopping. The same holds for tabemasu and tabemono (food), nomimasu and nomimono (drink). The -mono ending means "thing".

1. te imasu: happening right now

The て form plus imasu. The plain masu form states what you do; te imasu says you are in the middle of it.

I eat pasta
わたしは パスタを 食べます
Watashi wa pasuta o tabete imasu

わたしは パスタを 食べて います
Watashi wa ongaku o kiite imasu
I am listening to music
わたしは 音楽を 聞いて います
Watashi wa hon o yonde imasu
I am reading a book
わたしは 本を 読んで います
Watashi wa tegami o kaite imasu
I am writing a letter
わたしは 手紙を 書いて います
Watashi wa kouen de hashitte imasu
I am running in the park
わたしは 公園で 走って います
Kinou no kuji wa geemu o shite imashita
At nine yesterday I was playing a game imashita puts the whole thing in the past
A game is played with suru

Asobu takes the place it happens in, marked with de: kouen de asobimasu. It does not take a game as its object. A game is geemu o shimasu, so geemu o shite imashita rather than geemu o asonde imashita.

te imasu: an ongoing state

The same form also covers something true over a stretch of time rather than at this instant. English uses a different tense for each; Japanese does not.

Nihongo o benkyou shite imasu
I am studying Japanese, and have been for a while
日本語を 勉強して います
Nihongo o benkyou shite imashita
I used to study Japanese
日本語を 勉強して いました
Watashi wa gakkou ni itte imasu
I go to school, meaning I am a student
わたしは 学校に 行って います
Watashi wa Tokyo ni sunde imasu
I live in Tokyo
わたしは 東京に 住んで います
One form, two readings

Which reading you get depends on the verb, not the grammar. Taberu takes time to do, so tabete imasu is mid-action. Sumu happens in an instant and then lasts, so sunde imasu is the state that followed, not the act of moving in.

Existence: imasu and arimasu

Imasu is existence for people and animals. Things take arimasu.

Watashi wa kouen ni imasu
I am in the park
わたしは 公園に います
Kouen de asonde imasu
I am playing in the park
公園で 遊んで います
Presence takes ni, action takes de

Kouen ni imasu is where you are. Kouen de asonde imasu is where the playing is happening. Same park, different particle, because the verb is doing a different job.

2. te, te, masu: connecting verbs

Chains actions in the order they happen. Every verb but the last takes the て form, and only the final verb carries the tense.

koto: verbs as nouns

Add koto to a plain verb and it becomes a noun: eating, reading, swimming. This is how you say you like doing something.

Taberu koto ga suki desu
I like eating

Watashi no mokuhyou wa nihongo de hanasu koto desu
My goal is to speak in Japanese
Why ga and not o

With suki, the thing you like takes ga, not o: Sushi ga suki desu. In English "I like sushi" makes sushi feel like the object. In Japanese it is the subject of "is liked".

The voice diary

Koe nikki, a spoken diary. Six questions in order, every day. It runs you through past, present and future in one pass. The entries themselves are in the diary.

#QuestionAnswer with
1Hizuke: what is today's date?Kyou wa ku-gatsu juu-hachi nichi desu.
2Tenki: how is the weather?Kyou wa ame desu. / Kyou wa sukoshi atsui desu.
3Kinou: what did you do yesterday?Kinou wa geemu o shimashita.
4Kyou: what did you do today?Asa shichi-ji ni okimashita.
5Kimochi: how do you feel?Kyou wa tanoshikatta desu.
6Ashita: what will you do tomorrow?Ashita wa suupaa ni ikimasu.
